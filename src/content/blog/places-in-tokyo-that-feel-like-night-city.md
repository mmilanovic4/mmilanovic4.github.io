Night City is in California, but part of it came from Tokyo. When CD
Projekt Red built it, the team
[named Tokyo](https://domusweb.it/en/architecture/gallery/2020/12/21/night-city-how-the-cyberpunk-2077s-megalopolis-was-built.html)
among the real cities it took references from, next to Los Angeles, Detroit
and Hong Kong.

_Cyberpunk 2077_ is one of my favorite games and I played it on the Switch
2 before the trip. In Tokyo I kept running into pieces of it.

![Godzilla over Kabukichō](/blog/kabukicho.webp "1200x1600")

The borrowing started long before the game.

## Where the future came from

The name was in Japan first. William Gibson's _Neuromancer_ came out in
1984 and opens in Chiba, where Night City is the district on the edge of
town in which nothing is legal and most of it is tolerated.

Four years later Mike Pondsmith published _Cyberpunk_, the tabletop game
2077 is built on. He hadn't read _Neuromancer_ yet — his sources were
Walter Jon Williams' _Hardwired_ and _Blade Runner_ — and he still ended up
with a Night City of his own on the California coast. One of the
corporations running his world, Arasaka, is Japanese and headquartered in
Tokyo. Night City only gets a branch office — with a tower attached.

That was the eighties. In 1989 alone, Sony bought Columbia Pictures and
Mitsubishi Estate bought Rockefeller Center, and the future looked like it
would be run from Tokyo. Across the river from Asakusa, the golden flame
Philippe Starck put on top of the Asahi building went up the same year.

![The Asahi flame and the Skytree, from Asakusa](/blog/asakusa-skytree.webp "1200x1600")

Science fiction wrote that future down and kept it long after the economy
changed its mind.

## Shinjuku

Shinjuku Station splits the neighborhood in two and both halves are in the
game.

East of the station is Kabukichō — karaoke, pachinko, bars and signs
stacked up the side of every building all the way to the roof. My favorite
shisha bar is on the top floor of one of them. Since 2015, a Godzilla head
has been looking down at all of it from the Shinjuku Toho Building.

In Night City it's Japantown in Westbrook: gaming parlors, street food,
capsule hotels and Jig-Jig Street, with the Tyger Claws running the lot.
The district next door in Watson is called Kabuki.

Across from the east exit, a giant cat has been leaning out of a curved
screen since 2021. It's a flat screen pretending to be 3D, and from the
crowd below the trick works. Standing under it at night is a scene straight
out of _Blade Runner 2049_.

![The east exit cat](/blog/shinjuku-cat.mp4 "720x1280")

West of the station, Omoide Yokochō is a few narrow alleys of yakitori
counters and bars with a handful of seats each. It started as a black
market in the late 1940s, selling food and drinks that were hard to find
anywhere else after the war. Kabuki is Night City's biggest black market.
Omoide Yokochō started the same way and ended up selling yakitori.

![Kabukichō](/blog/kabukicho-neon.webp "1200x1600")
![Outside Omoide Yokochō](/blog/shinjuku-neon.webp "1200x1600")

Further west the signs give way to towers. The Tokyo Metropolitan
Government Building is Kenzo Tange's: 48 floors, split into two towers at
the top, opened in 1991. At night it's lit in colors that would fit right
in over Corpo Plaza.

In Night City, a building like that belongs to Arasaka. In Tokyo, it's city
hall.

![Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building](/blog/tokyo-city-hall.webp "1200x1600")

## Shibuya

Shibuya is the one people come to watch. Every time the lights change, the
cars stop in every direction at once and the crossing in front of the
station fills from all sides, under screens that never stop playing ads.

_Cyberpunk 2077_ has a setting called **Crowd Density**. Shibuya doesn't
have a setting. It's on high all the time.

![Shibuya](/blog/shibuya-screens.webp "1200x1600")
![Screens at dusk](/blog/shibuya-dusk.webp "1200x1600")

## Pacifica, done right

Pacifica is the one I had to leave Tokyo for.

In the game, it's Night City's stretch of coast: a resort district whose
investors pulled out halfway through building it. The hotels and malls were
left half-finished and the Voodoo Boys moved in.

Kamakura is about an hour from Tokyo by train. It sits on Sagami Bay, which
opens onto the same ocean. The beach at Yuigahama had surfers walking out
with their boards. The hills behind it are temples, and the Great Buddha
has sat in the open since 1498, when a tsunami washed away the hall built
around it.

Night City's coast lost its investors and gave up. Kamakura's lost a roof
and kept the Buddha.

![Yuigahama beach, Kamakura](/blog/yuigahama.webp "1600x1200")

## Kept the neon

For three years, the trains in Night City were scenery. They ran overhead
and you couldn't get on. Patch 2.1 opened the NCART metro in December 2023
— five lines, 19 stations — but the city is still built around the car.

Tokyo is built around the train. The metro, JR, the private lines, the
train to Kamakura — one card covers all of them, which is
[a post of its own](/blog/suica-twenty-years-ahead).

![Neon behind the wires](/blog/shinjuku-wires.webp "1392x1600")

Everything cyberpunk took from Tokyo is still there: the neon, the signs
climbing the buildings, the towers and the alleys that started as black
markets. What it didn't take is the part that keeps the city running.

Night City kept the neon and patched in the trains.
